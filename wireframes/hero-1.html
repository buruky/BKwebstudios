Options (picked: cutout, whole thing flips on hover, stack of prints, peek then go)

A · Photo shape

1 · Plain panel. Edge-to-edge photo, no border. Cleanest, most "Filmmaker".

2 · Print with a thin border. The film finish you liked, without becoming a Polaroid prop.

My pick

3 · Rounded card. Softer and friendlier; less film feel.

4 · Cutout breaking the frame. You stand in a low box, your head and thumbs up rising out of it. Most dramatic; the beanie edge needs careful cutting.

B · Black-and-white to color

1 · Flip on hover. Desktop: hovering turns the print over like a real photo (a 3D flip, 0.5s) and the back is the color version. Phone: tap the photo to flip it.

My pick

2 · Develop on scroll. As the visitor scrolls the first ~200px, the print spins a little and color fades in, like it's developing. Works the same on phones, no tap needed.

3 · Both. Hover flips on desktop; scrolling develops on phones.

C · Style switcher (easy to understand, clearly visible)

A · Stack of prints. Each style is a print; click the stack and the next one slides to the top. Matches the photo; the "coming soon" styles are blank prints. Needs a small "01 / 05" under it so it's obvious.

B · Number tabs. 01 to 05 in a row, the current one filled chicory. The most obvious; less charming.

C · Counter pill. "‹ 01 / 05 ›" in the date-stamp style, ember outline. Compact and clear, sits in the credits row.

0ms: everything is already in place and readable → 0–400ms: the whole hero fades up from 0 to 100% (one quick fade, ease-out) while the print settles from 0° to its 3° tilt. That's it: under half a second, per your pick. Reduced motion: no fade, no tilt.
